# Adrian Liston colleague writing

Use this before drafting any message or email to colleagues (a team chat, a working-group email, or a 1:1 note to someone you already work with). Match the voice, not the identity: sign with the sender’s own name, never as Adrian.

## Structure

1. Greeting: `Hi everyone,` or `Hi all,` for a group; `Dear all,` only if the topic is formal or serious; `Hi [Name],` for one person.
2. Lead with the point in the first sentence (what you need, and when or where if that matters).
3. Put logistics in one line if there are any (time, place, link).
4. Add one or two sentences on why it matters to the reader.
5. Keep the whole note short: usually 2 to 6 sentences. A forward or a pointer gets one framing line, then the ask.
6. Sign off warmly: `Cheers,` for everyday notes; `Regards,` for cancellations or more formal news; a supportive close when the topic is hard.

## Tone

- Warm, plain, conversational English. Contractions are fine.
- Invitational, not commanding: invite people in (`why not…`, `it would be great if…`) instead of issuing orders.
- Honest and lightly self-aware when that helps (`if you are like me…`), without making the note about yourself.
- Frame a new ask as low-risk when you can: best case they gain something; otherwise they can continue as before.
- Credit people by name when they did the work. Be inclusive (timing, access, people who may not drink or may have other commitments).
- For a serious topic, be direct, say the important part more than once, and point to a concrete next step or resource.
- For a 1:1 technical answer: give the answer first, then a short reason (a simple example if it helps), then what you actually do or recommend.
- Leave an open door: they can reply with times, questions, or a better option.

## Do not

- Do not pad with background they already know.
- Do not stack several asks in one note if one clear ask will do.
- Do not sound like a help-desk template or a formal policy memo unless the subject truly requires it.
