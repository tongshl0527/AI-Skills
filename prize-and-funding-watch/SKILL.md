# Prize and funding watch

Use this whenever a search, event, email, or conversation turns up a prize, award, grant, scholarship, or other funding that might help the user's studies, research, career, or projects — or when they ask you to watch for those so they do not miss an application.

## Capture before you share
For each find, write down only what a source supports, and label facts VERIFIED or INFERRED:
- name and organizer
- what it rewards and who is eligible
- prize or amount, if stated
- how people enter: open application, nomination, invitation after a grade or result, or another route
- application window, deadline, or the date it is awarded (do not treat an award ceremony as an application deadline)
- official URL
- why it might help, tied to the user's stated goals
- effort and whether they are eligible now or only after a future course, degree, or result

Do not invent officer names, deadlines, or eligibility. Do not store private emails, phone numbers, or home addresses in contact records; point to the official page instead.

## Do not act on the user's behalf
Do not apply, register, nominate anyone, or contact the organizer unless the user explicitly asked you to take that step.

## Triage before telling the user
Do not notify the user about every find. Send a short brief to their Chief of Staff (or coordinating assistant) with: what it is, fit to their goals, deadline urgency (flag anything due within 7 days), whether they can apply now or only later, and a recommendation: record-and-watch, tell them now, or skip.

If the user asked about a specific event or prize in the conversation you are already in, you may answer that question there. Still send the structured find to Chief of Staff so it can be triaged for recording and reminders.

## After it is marked helpful
If Chief of Staff (or the user) says the find is helpful:
1. Record it as an opportunity, with the details above and the next review or reminder date.
2. For a recurring annual prize, store the eligibility trigger and typical cycle (for example, awarded each November for the previous academic year), not a false "apply this week" date.
3. Remind the user in time to prepare or apply — earlier if they first need a grade, a nomination, or a piece of work.
4. If they are not eligible yet, remind them when the trigger is near (course enrollment, internship report, clinical assignment), not as if they can apply today.

If it is marked not helpful, keep a closed or passed note so it is not repeatedly re-pitched, unless the terms change.
