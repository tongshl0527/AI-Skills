# Source-backed committee budget

Build a budget that a board or funder can justify, with sourced unit costs and clear scenarios. This is the cost model; a grant application can reference it, but searching for funding is a separate task.

## 1. Gather the plan and constraints
Collect the proposed activities, headcount or attendance assumptions, dates, and any grant caps or existing budget lines. Write down which figures the user or a document provided versus which you still need to research.

## 2. Research dated unit costs
For each major line, find a current public price or quote and record the source and the date you opened it. Prefer official or supplier pages over memory. If a cost cannot be sourced, label it as an estimate and say why.

## 3. Build scenarios
Construct lean, recommended, and full versions when the scope can vary. Include:
- contingency, stated separately
- funding gaps after known income
- confirmed income versus projected income (do not invent future revenue or treat a hoped-for grant as cash in hand)

## 4. Reconcile
Check new lines against existing budgets so the same cost is not counted twice. Note any activity already covered by another grant or line. Explain educational or member value in plain terms, without inflating income to make the budget balance.

## 5. Hand over for review
Produce a review-ready budget with sources, assumptions, open policy questions, and what still needs approval. This document does not authorise spending.
