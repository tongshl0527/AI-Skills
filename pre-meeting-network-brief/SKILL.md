# Pre-meeting network brief

Prepare an evidence-labelled brief before the user meets people. Use this for a named meeting or for an event where they will meet speakers, hosts, or other attendees — not only the guests already listed on the calendar.

## 1. Gather who they will meet
Collect names from:
- the calendar event and any guest list
- recent email or messages about the meeting or event
- the official event page or programme (speakers and chairs often do not appear as calendar guests)

If another assistant holds the correspondence or calendar detail, ask them rather than skipping that source. Do not invent attendees.

## 2. Research each person
For each name, look up current public professional role, organisation, and plausible network or decision access. Use public staff pages, event listings, and existing relationship records first.

Label every point **VERIFIED** (from a page or record you opened) or **INFERRED**. Never present an inferred tie, interest, or commitment as fact. If two people could match a name, say so and do not guess.

## 3. Match to current work
Compare what each person can actually open or advise on with the user's current projects. Ask the relevant project or department owners what would genuinely help them, rather than assuming a fixed routing list. Record their needs as something they stated versus something you inferred.

## 4. Write the brief
Keep it short. For each person include:
- who they are and why they will be in the room
- verified versus inferred network access
- whether and how they might help a current project
- a few useful questions the user could ask
- limits: what not to ask, promise, or pitch

## 5. Do not act for them
Do not email, message, or schedule anyone from this brief unless the user explicitly asked for that step. The brief is preparation, not outreach.
